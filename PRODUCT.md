# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Synthetic organic chemists — PhD students, postdocs, and principal investigators — who run bench chemistry and care about whether published procedures actually work. They visit to read peer reviews before attempting a synthesis, and to leave honest assessments after they try one. Secondary audience: editors and publishers monitoring reproducibility signals. Users are technically literate but not designers; they expect a tool that gets out of the way and lets the science speak.

## Product Purpose

ChemRepro is a community reproducibility rating platform for synthetic chemistry papers. It exists because published procedures often fail in other labs and there is no structured, searchable record of those failures. Success looks like: a chemist searches a DOI before attempting a procedure, reads credible peer assessments, and either proceeds with confidence or avoids a dead end. Credibility is the core asset — every design decision either builds or erodes it.

## Brand Personality

Trusted · Approachable · Accessible

The platform must feel as credible as a journal, as usable as a modern tool, and as welcoming as a collegial department hallway. It should never feel like a startup, never feel like social media, and never feel like an academic database from 2009.

## References

- **PubPeer**: Right tone — uncompromising scientific honesty, no marketing gloss, community ownership. Take the seriousness; avoid the visual austerity.
- **Linear**: Right craft — clean, fast, precise product UI; considered typography; no unnecessary chrome. Take the polish and focus; adapt warmth for a scientific audience.

## Anti-references

- **ResearchGate**: Cluttered, ad-heavy, visually dated. Never look like a social network trying to monetise academics.
- **Generic SaaS landing pages**: Avoid stock photography of scientists, gradient hero blobs, or "Reproducibility as a Service" marketing language.
- **Wikipedia / legacy academic databases**: Functional but visually joyless. ChemRepro should feel built with intention.

## Design Principles

1. **Credibility is the interface**: Every element — typography weight, whitespace, information hierarchy — should reinforce that this is a serious scientific record, not a comment section.
2. **Data over decoration**: Scores, observations, and DOIs are the content. Design surfaces them clearly; it doesn't compete with them.
3. **Accessible by default**: WCAG 2.1 AA throughout. The platform serves an international academic community across devices and contexts.
4. **Approachable, not dumbed down**: Reduce friction without simplifying the science. A first-year PhD and a tenured professor should both feel at home.
5. **Earn trust at every touchpoint**: Consistent spacing, readable type, deliberate colour use. Sloppiness signals untrustworthiness in a credibility-dependent product.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigable throughout. All interactive elements have visible focus states. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning (scores use text labels alongside colour). Reduced-motion media query respected for all animations.
